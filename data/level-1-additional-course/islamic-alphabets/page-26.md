# Yelling: Controlling Anger and Speech

## Problem with Yelling

Yelling or shouting in anger is discouraged in our faith. It harms relationships, creates tension, and often leads to regret after the anger subsides.

## Anger Management

Our Prophet taught us specific ways to control anger. He said, "If one of you becomes angry while standing, let him sit down. If the anger does not leave him, let him lie down."

## The Strong Person

The Prophet said that the strong person is not the one who defeats others in wrestling, but the one who controls himself at the time of anger. Self-control is a sign of true strength for us.

## Seeking Refuge from Shaytan

When angry, we should seek refuge in Allah from Shaytan by saying "A'udhu billahi min ash-shaytan ir-rajim." Anger is a spark from Shaytan that can lead us to harmful actions.

## Making Wudu When Angry

The Prophet advised us to make wudu when angry, as water cools the fire of anger. This practical step helps calm our minds and prevents saying or doing something regrettable.

## Speaking Softly

Our faith encourages us to speak softly and politely, even when upset. The Quran describes the believers as those who speak gently and avoid harsh words.

## Silence as a Remedy

The Prophet said, "Whoever remains silent will be saved." Sometimes the best response to anger is silence until our emotions settle.

## Forgiveness Over Yelling

Our faith teaches us forgiveness over retaliation. Letting go of anger and forgiving others brings us greater reward and peace than yelling or seeking revenge.

## Common Mistakes

Some among us think yelling shows strength or gets things done faster. Others habitually raise their voices in arguments without realizing the harm it causes to relationships.
