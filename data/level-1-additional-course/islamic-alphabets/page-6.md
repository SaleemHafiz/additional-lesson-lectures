# Eid: Celebrations of Gratitude

## Meaning of Eid

Eid is our festival that marks the completion of worship. We have two main Eids: Eid al-Fitr after Ramadan and Eid al-Adha after Hajj.

## Eid al-Fitr

Eid al-Fitr is celebrated on the first day of Shawwal after Ramadan. It is a day of joy, gratitude, and charity, thanking Allah for the strength He gave us during fasting.

## Eid al-Adha

Eid al-Adha occurs on the 10th of Dhul Hijjah during Hajj. It commemorates Prophet Ibrahim's willingness to sacrifice his son and involves sacrificing an animal.

## Acts of Eid

Eid begins with a special congregational prayer. We wear our best clothes, recite takbeer, visit family, give gifts, and share food with others.

## Charity on Eid

On Eid al-Fitr, we give Zakat al-Fitr before the Eid prayer to help the poor celebrate. On Eid al-Adha, meat from the sacrifice is shared with family, neighbors, and the needy.

## Eid Spirit

Eid teaches us gratitude, generosity, and unity. It strengthens family bonds and spreads happiness in our community.

## Sunnah of Eid

The Prophet would take a bath, eat dates before Eid prayer, go to the prayer ground, and return by a different route. These sunnah practices bring blessings.

## Eid and Non-Muslims

We are encouraged to show kindness and good relations with non-Muslims during Eid. Sharing food and greetings with neighbors builds peace and understanding.

## Common Mistakes

Some among us celebrate Eid with extravagance and waste while forgetting the poor. Others neglect the Eid prayer or engage in un-Islamic activities during celebrations.
