# Islamic Manners: Cleanliness, Eating Etiquette, and Good Conduct

## Introduction

* Islamic teachings guide Muslims in every aspect of daily life, including cleanliness, eating, and social manners.
* These manners are part of faith and help a person develop discipline, respect, and gratitude.
* The exercises highlight key principles related to hygiene, eating etiquette, and correct behavior in Islam.

## Cleanliness as Part of Faith

* Cleanliness is an important part of faith, as stated in authentic Islamic teachings that emphasize purity.
* Islam encourages physical cleanliness because it reflects inner discipline and respect for others.
* Personal hygiene practices such as washing, grooming, and oral care are part of daily Sunnah.
* Neglecting cleanliness contradicts the Islamic emphasis on purity and self-care.

## Sunnah of Oral Hygiene

* The Prophet Muhammad ﷺ encouraged the use of the miswak for cleaning the teeth.
* Miswak is a natural tool used in Sunnah, while modern toothbrushes are also permissible for cleanliness.
* Oral hygiene is encouraged in Islam because it supports both health and worship.
* Maintaining clean breath and teeth is part of respecting others in social interaction.

## Manners of Eating and Drinking

* A Muslim should begin eating by saying the name of Allah and end with gratitude to Him.
* Eating should be done in moderation without greed or excess.
* It is recommended to serve elders first as a sign of respect.
* A person should use the right hand while eating and drinking as taught in Islamic manners.

## Social Manners During Meals

* A Muslim should avoid watching others while they eat to prevent discomfort or embarrassment.
* A Muslim should serve food fairly and respectfully, especially prioritizing elders.
* A Muslim should not behave greedily or take more than their fair share in group meals.
* Good manners during meals strengthen harmony and respect among people.

## Halal and Haram Food Awareness

* Muslims are required to consume only halal food as commanded by Allah.
* Haram foods are prohibited, while halal foods are abundant and sufficient for all needs.
* Eating halal food is an act of obedience and earns reward from Allah.
* Respecting dietary laws strengthens awareness of Islamic boundaries.

## General Hygiene Practices in Islam

* Rinsing the mouth after eating is encouraged for cleanliness and freshness.
* Keeping nails trimmed is part of personal hygiene and should not be neglected.
* Regular bathing is important for maintaining cleanliness and good health.
* Wearing clean and neat clothing reflects respect for oneself and others.

## Evaluation of Common Behaviors

* Using miswak for brushing teeth is a Sunnah practice and therefore good.
* Not clipping nails regularly is considered poor hygiene and discouraged.
* Eating greedily is a bad manner because it shows lack of self-control.
* Serving elders first is a good manner as it reflects respect and humility.
* Looking at others while they eat is inappropriate and may cause discomfort.

## True or False Clarifications

* Cleanliness is part of faith, making the statement true.
* Miswak is made from natural twigs, not soft plastic bristles, making that statement false.
* Rinsing the mouth before or after eating is a good practice for hygiene, making that statement true.
* Holding drinking vessels with the right hand is part of proper Islamic etiquette, so the opposite claim is false.
* Halal foods are permitted in Islam, so claiming they are forbidden is false.

## Key Takeaways

* Cleanliness is a central value in Islam and closely linked to faith.
* Eating manners reflect respect, humility, and obedience to Allah.
* Miswak and hygiene practices are part of the Prophet’s Sunnah.
* Halal and respectful behavior in daily life strengthens character and discipline.

## Final Reminder

* Islamic manners are not only rules but a way of building a clean, respectful, and balanced life.
* A Muslim should apply these teachings consistently in daily habits to gain both spiritual and social benefit.
