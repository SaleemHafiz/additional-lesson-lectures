# Islamic Manners: Cleanliness, Eating Etiquette, and Good Conduct

## Introduction

* Islam guides all daily actions including hygiene, eating, and social behavior.
* These manners are part of faith and build discipline, respect, and gratitude.

## Cleanliness as Part of Faith

* Cleanliness is closely linked to faith and personal discipline.
* Islam encourages hygiene, grooming, and purity as daily practice.

## Sunnah of Oral Hygiene

* The Prophet ﷺ encouraged miswak for cleaning teeth.
* Toothbrush use is also allowed as it serves the same purpose.

## Manners of Eating and Drinking

* Begin with Allah’s name and end with gratitude.
* Eat in moderation, use the right hand, and serve elders first.

## Social Manners During Meals

* Avoid staring at others while they eat.
* Share food fairly and behave politely in group meals.

## Halal and Haram Food Awareness

* Muslims must eat only halal food as obedience to Allah.
* Halal food is abundant and brings reward when eaten correctly.

## General Hygiene Practices in Islam

* Rinse mouth, trim nails, bathe regularly, and wear clean clothes.
* Hygiene is part of health, discipline, and respect.

## Evaluation of Common Behaviors

* Miswak use and serving elders are good manners.
* Greed, long nails, and poor hygiene are bad behaviors.

## True or False Clarifications

* Cleanliness is part of faith — true.
* Miswak is natural — true, and halal rules are correct as stated.

## Key Takeaways

* Cleanliness and manners are core Islamic values.
* Eating etiquette reflects respect, humility, and obedience.

## Final Reminder

* Islamic manners shape a clean and respectful lifestyle.
* Consistent practice brings both spiritual and social benefit.
